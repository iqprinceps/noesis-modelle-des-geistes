# Claim, Tone and Audio-Visual Synchronization Standard

## Purpose

Every English episode is judged as one continuous viewer experience. Narration,
cards, documents, images, clips, music, sound effects and later recaps must carry
the same evidentiary meaning and emotional direction. Individually accurate
elements still fail when their combination creates a contradiction, dismisses
the story's own question or visually implies something the narration does not.

This is a reasoning standard, not a fixed wording or asset quota. Each episode
receives its own viewer-side review.

## Positive evidence language

State what the evidence establishes first. Then name the question that remains.

Useful pattern:

> The paralysis is measurable. Why it so often feels like another presence is
> the question that remains.

Avoid building mystery and then weakening it with repetitive defensive language
such as:

- `just a hallucination`;
- `merely a dream`;
- `not proof` as a recurring card or refrain;
- `nothing supernatural happened` when the evidence does not settle that wider
  interpretation;
- categorical entity imagery when the narration presents only a possibility.

Accuracy is not achieved by adding disclaimers everywhere. It is achieved by
precise attribution, positive statements of established facts and grammatically
clear possibilities or questions where interpretation remains open.

## Beat-level consistency matrix

Before voice lock and again before picture lock, every consequential beat is
reviewed across these fields:

| Field | Review question |
|---|---|
| Spoken line | What exactly does the viewer hear? |
| Positive claim | What has the episode established at this point? |
| Open question/theory | What is possible, reported or unresolved rather than established? |
| Image/clip/document | Does the visible subject match the spoken subject and evidentiary status? |
| Card text | Is it equally strong and equally precise, without becoming more dismissive or more certain? |
| SFX/music | Does the sound embody the reported experience without asserting an external cause as fact? |
| Callback/recap | Does the later wording preserve rather than reverse the earlier meaning? |
| Risk | Could the combination create a self-contradiction, unwanted implication or tonal own goal? |

If a conflict exists, correct the canonical script, card, cue or asset. Do not
cover the conflict with an additional disclaimer.

## Semantic picture synchronization

- A named real person receives a rights-safe authentic identity/source anchor or
  an explicitly non-identifying action context, never an invented likeness.
- A named experiment receives the actual paper, apparatus or result context
  before reconstruction or abstraction.
- A spoken number or result must visibly land when it is central to the story.
- Abstract or mystical imagery visualizes a reported inner experience or theory;
  it does not silently convert that theory into evidence.
- A callback uses a new source, viewpoint, action or later state and keeps the
  same claim boundary.
- Cuts, transformations and highlights should align with the word or sentence
  that changes meaning, not arrive arbitrarily early or late.

## SFX and music synchronization

- Sound has a narrative function: location, physical sensation, transition,
  evidence, subjectivity, tension or release.
- Avoid arbitrary horror impacts, constant drone beds and repeated stingers.
- Subjective footsteps, breath, pressure or room tone may embody a reported
  experience. They must not be mixed as objective proof that an entity exists.
- Evidence/source beats benefit from clarity and controlled space.
- The largest mystery beat may use deliberate near-silence when absence creates
  more attention than another effect.
- Voice remains intelligible; ducking, transient timing and frequency masking
  are checked on the final mix rather than assumed from stems.

## Pre-render audit

The episode owner selects its consequential beats and records the lightest
audio-video synchronization review that can expose a real contradiction. It
normally covers the episode-specific equivalents of:

- the hook and first factual anchor;
- the main explanatory mechanism;
- the primary experiment or source result;
- the central viewer interaction;
- the mystery or theory peak;
- the final callback and next-episode handoff.

Review the actual rendered sequence, not only the cue sheet. If wording changes,
regenerate all affected voice, cards, subtitles, cues and metadata from the
canonical source so stale contradictory derivatives cannot survive.
