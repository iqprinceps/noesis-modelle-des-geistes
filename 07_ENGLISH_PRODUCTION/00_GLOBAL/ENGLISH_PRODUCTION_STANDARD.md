# English Production Standard

## Scope and precedence

For every English edition, the order of authority is:

1. current explicit channel-owner decisions;
2. the episode's canonical script, editorial map, and preproduction visual lock;
3. the standards in `07_ENGLISH_PRODUCTION/00_GLOBAL`;
4. older German standards and masters as technical reference only.

Legacy German targets for word count, binary CTAs, card quantity, shot quantity,
clip quantity, language, and permanent labeling do not bind the English editions.
Factual attribution, source/license clearance, identity accuracy, and technical
delivery safety remain hard requirements.

## Editorial rule

English editions are adaptations, not sentence-by-sentence translations.
Facts and evidentiary boundaries remain locked to the German source. Hooks,
syntax, explanations, and transitions may be rewritten for natural English.

## Hook review

The opening is judged by whether it quickly creates a concrete reason to stay.
Useful ingredients during the first story movement are:

1. a concrete impossible action or human contradiction;
2. a primary source, date, patent, report, or measured number;
3. the contradiction inside that source;
4. the question the episode will answer.

Avoid a logo pre-roll when it delays the story. A first visual change around 2.5
seconds and a verifiable reality anchor within roughly 15 seconds are diagnostic
references, not universal deadlines. The episode map decides whether that
anchor is an original source, a measured number, an authentic identity, or a
documented object. Episode-specific hook timing overrides a generic deadline.

## Evidence language

Production manifests use five internal classifications:

- `ARCHIVE`
- `ORIGINAL DOCUMENT`
- `RECONSTRUCTION`
- `EXPLANATORY MODEL`
- `INNER / HYPOTHESIS`

These classifications are not persistent viewer-facing badges. `INNER / HYPOTHESIS`
never appears as visible text. Reconstructions are distinguished primarily by a
coherent cinematic language; an optional subtle disclosure may appear for 1.5–2.0
seconds at the first entry into a continuous reconstruction block. Original portraits
and documents establish identity; reconstruction supplies action. Source/date text
appears only when it adds evidence, not as permanent furniture.

## Language and voice

- Use idiomatic international English.
- Avoid literal German syntax and stacked rhetorical antitheses.
- Prefer direct statements over scripted denial-and-reversal patterns such as
  `This is not X; it is Y`. Use negation only when the absent or disproven fact
  itself matters to the viewer.
- Write dates and numbers so the selected voice reads them naturally.
- Maintain a pronunciation sheet for every proper name.
- Review all generated takes before alignment. Automatic dubbing is not the
  production master.

## Cross-modal consistency

Narration, cards, documents, visuals, music, SFX, subtitles and recaps follow
`CLAIM_TONE_SYNC_STANDARD.md`. They must preserve one evidentiary meaning and
emotional direction. Factual boundaries are expressed positively and precisely;
they are not repeated as dismissive disclaimers that undermine the episode's
own unresolved question.

## Interaction

Do not interrupt evidence with a generic request for engagement. An episode-specific
decision may sit at a central turn when answering it makes the viewer participate in
the investigation. A follow/subscribe request stays after the story payoff.

## Localization

Only German explanatory cards selected for the English cue sheet are rebuilt.
Original-language source documents remain original, with a concise English
translation beside or below the relevant line. Never replace a primary source
with a translated facsimile that could be mistaken for the original.

## Original-asset acquisition

Before generating replacements, review the episode for every authentic visual
anchor that could materially increase trust, specificity or historical texture.
Depending on the individual story, this may include:

- rights-safe portraits and biographical identity images;
- laboratories, institutes, apparatus, experiments and scientific photographs;
- books, papers, catalog records, notebooks, correspondence and field notes;
- court records, manuscripts, depositions and other primary documents;
- maps, locations, buildings, material culture and period objects;
- contemporary illustrations, prints, cartoons, newspaper imagery and artworks
  with a documented connection to the spoken subject.

This is a relevance search, not an archive quota. Prefer primary sources,
official archives, museums, libraries, universities, journals and clearly
licensed collections. Every selected asset records its origin, creator/date,
license or use basis, retrieval date, local path, content hash, spoken cue and
editorial purpose.

Similarity is not identity. A generic historic laboratory cannot be presented
as the named experiment, and a generic witch or nightmare image cannot be
presented as a specific local tradition without source support. When no honest
original exists, record the gap and use a clearly contextual reconstruction or
abstract interpretation instead of inventing archive evidence.

## Delivery and packaging

- 1920 x 1080, 30 fps
- 48 kHz stereo
- final loudness: -14 LUFS +/- 0.5
- true peak: no higher than -0.8 dBTP
- English SRT and VTT
- episode-specific end-card allowance based on the final platform need
- enough materially different thumbnail concepts to make a real choice, each
  checked at mobile size
- all still-image camera motion passes the project-wide technical smooth-motion
  gate in `VISUAL_RETENTION_STANDARD.md`; a clean contact sheet alone cannot
  approve motion cadence
