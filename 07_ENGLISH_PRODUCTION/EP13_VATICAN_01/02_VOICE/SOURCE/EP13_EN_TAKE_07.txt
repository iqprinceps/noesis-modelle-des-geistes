A reported vision became a handwritten page.

The page became a sealed object.

The object entered an archive and gained power over people who were never allowed to read it.

And when it was finally opened, it gave three different readers three different truths.

That is the pattern this series follows.

An institution takes something uncertain, and gives it a form.

Here, the form was an envelope.

The next form is much larger.

In July 1530, eighty-three of the most powerful men in England signed a single sheet of parchment and hung eighty-one seals from it in metal cases.

They were trying to force a pope to end their king's marriage.

He refused.

Within four years England broke with Rome, and the marriage ended without him.

The letter failed at everything it was written to do.

It is still in Rome, seals attached, almost five hundred years later.

Other parts of that same archive were carried over the Alps by Napoleon and never came back. Some of it was sold by weight, as waste paper.
