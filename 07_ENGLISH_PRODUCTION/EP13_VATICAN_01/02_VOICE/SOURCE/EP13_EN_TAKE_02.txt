The reported apparitions at Fatima ran through the summer of 1917. The children spoke about prayer, about war, about Russia, and about things they would not describe in full.

Twenty-seven years later, on the third of January, 1944, one of them sat down and wrote the last part out.

Lucia dos Santos was the only one of the three still alive.

And the moment she put the pencil down, the story changed shape.

A reported vision became an object.

Paper. Ink. A sealed envelope that can be carried, stored, guarded, and handed to one person at a time.

In 1957 the envelope was brought to Rome and placed in the archive of the Holy Office.

Hold that situation in your mind for a second, because it is a strange one.

A document exists.

Its author is alive and can be asked about it.

The men permitted to read it are known by name.

And everyone else is told only that it is there.

In 1959, John the Twenty-Third read it.

He did not publish it.

Six years later, Paul the Sixth read it.

He sent it back to the archive.
