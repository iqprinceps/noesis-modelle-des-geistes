# EP13_EN — Reveal Act Treatment

The problem this document solves: the episode's strongest act delivers what John
Paul II read, and the manuscript facsimile is `REFERENCE_ONLY`. Vatican portal
terms do not grant the reuse this production needs, and two search passes found
no open-licence facsimile of those photographs.

Covers roughly 4:50 to 6:30 in the voice script, from `The Vatican published the
manuscript in the year 2000` to `The man reading it in July 1981 did.`

## The reframe that makes this work

The absence is not a hole to patch. It is the subject.

For fifty-six years the power of this document came from the fact that almost
nobody could look at it. Two popes read it and said nothing, and the empty space
filled with wars and endings. An episode about a text whose force came from being
unseen does not need to show the paper. It needs to make the viewer feel the
reading.

Check the script and it never promises a document shot:

> So we can read exactly what he read.
> Read it the way he had to. From a hospital bed. Two months after somebody shot you.

That promises the text and the position of the reader. Both are deliverable.

The act is therefore carried by the vision, not by the page.

## Shot logic

**1. The publication beat.** `The Vatican published the manuscript in the year
2000, as a photographic facsimile with a translation and a commentary.`

Evidence card `EP13-A05` family: the fact of publication, the date, and the
locator to the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith document. Typography
only. No page image, no imitation of one.

**2. Position the reader.** `Read it the way he had to. From a hospital bed.`

Reconstruction: a room, a bed, hands, a single sheet held. The sheet is
`EP13-A03`: one page, roughly twenty-five lines, based on the attested physical
description of the manuscript. Framing keeps the writing below legibility at all
times, because the reconstruction must never appear to reproduce Sister Lucia's
hand. Shoot the reader, not the document.

**3. The vision, six images.** This is the body of the act and the reason it does
not need the facsimile. The Vatican text names them in order, so the sequence is
already written:

| Beat | Image | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Angel with a flaming sword; flames that look as though they will set the world on fire; the flames go out | The source has them extinguished on contact with the light from Our Lady. Play the extinguishing, it is the first reversal |
| 2 | A bishop dressed in white | Never a recognisable John Paul II. The whole point of the act is that the text names nobody |
| 3 | A large city, half in ruins | |
| 4 | Corpses he passes on the way | Restraint. Suggestion over depiction |
| 5 | A steep mountain, a large rough-hewn cross at the top | |
| 6 | Soldiers firing bullets and arrows; the figure falls; others die with him | The arrows are in the source and they are what make the image feel out of time. Keep them |

Available and deliberately unused: the angels beneath the cross gathering the
martyrs' blood in crystal vessels, and the angel's cry of `Penance, Penance,
Penance!`. Both are in the source. The act ends on the deaths so the turn lands.
They may be used as image without narration if the sequence needs a seventh beat.

**4. What the page never says.** Card `EP13-A05`. The card lists what the text
contains, then removes what it does not: no name, no date, no square, no car, no
survivor. A card can perform a subtraction. A photograph cannot. This is the one
moment where not having the manuscript is an advantage.

**5. The turn.** `And the bishop in white does not survive. / The man reading it
in July 1981 did.`

Cut from the falling figure in the reconstruction to the living man. This is the
strongest cut in the episode and it involves no document at all.

## Rules for the reconstruction

These follow `00_GLOBAL/ENGLISH_PRODUCTION_STANDARD.md`. Reconstructions are
distinguished by a coherent cinematic language, with an optional subtle
disclosure of 1.5 to 2.0 seconds at the first entry into a continuous
reconstruction block. No permanent badge, and `INNER / HYPOTHESIS` never appears
as visible text.

1. **Never imitate the hand.** No invented Portuguese script, no forged
   letterforms, no aged-paper texture derived from a photograph of the original.
   Legibility stays below reading threshold.
2. **The vision has its own world.** It must not share the grade, grain, lens or
   motion of the historical material. A described vision rendered like archive
   footage is the one failure that would damage the episode's credibility.
3. **No date-stamped realism.** The vision is undated in the source. Give it no
   period markers that would tie it to 1917, 1944 or 1981.
4. **The bishop in white stays unidentified.** No papal features, no recognisable
   vestments of a specific pontificate, no face held long enough to read.
5. **Cards carry precision, reconstruction carries feeling.** Every date, name
   and locator lives in typography. The reconstruction never asserts a fact.

## What this act costs

Six reconstruction images plus one page-object build plus two card families. No
licensing, no permission request, no dependency on a rights holder.

Compare the alternative: a permission request to the Holy See with an unknown
timeline and a likely refusal for commercial reuse, for an asset the act does not
structurally need.

**Recommendation: do not request permission for the manuscript.** Build the act
as specified. If an open-licence facsimile ever surfaces, it slots in at beat 1
as a short establishing shot without any other change.

## Consequence for the rest of the episode

The one asset the episode genuinely cannot replace is `EP13-HA01`, the crown. It
is the cold open, the payoff and the thumbnail, and it is the only rights-clear
photograph of the object in existence as far as two searches can establish.

It is a 1174 by 768 frame from Centro Televisivo Vaticano video. Verified,
downloaded, hashed. The bullet reads clearly: dull dark metal at the centre of
the crown, under the turquoise orb, surrounded by gold and brilliants. The
contradiction the episode is built on is legible in the frame as it stands.

Two constraints follow:

- upscaling to a 1920 by 1080 timeline is a 1.64x lift from a soft video still,
  which is acceptable with good treatment; a 4K finish is not advisable from this
  source;
- there is no macro. The bullet occupies a small part of the frame, and a
  full-frame detail would need roughly a 6x crop. Use a slow push and let the
  frame stay wide enough to hold. Do not promise a macro in the edit.

Worth one research pass before picture lock: the Commons file is a frame from a
CTV video that Commons records under CC BY 3.0. If the underlying video carries
the same licence, further frames could be extracted legitimately, which would
give the episode movement on its hero object instead of a single still. Verify
the video's own licence before assuming this.
