# EP13_EN — SEO and Packaging

## Title

`The Third Secret of Fatima: Why a Bullet Sits in Her Crown`

58 characters, so it survives the mobile truncation point. It follows the pattern
already used on this channel: the searchable term first, then the clause that
gives a reason to click.

**Why the title names the Third Secret when the script never does.** The script
avoids the phrase on purpose. It carries a heavy conspiracy framing, and the
episode is built to withhold the vision until the midpoint. Naming the envelope
is allowed in movement one; naming its contents is not, and the title does not.
What the phrase does is match the query people actually type. Leaving it out of
the title would hide the film from its own audience for no editorial gain.

## Thumbnail

Three variants in `../07_THUMBNAILS/`, built at 1920x1080 and proofed at 25 and
18.75 percent.

| Variant | Concept |
|---|---|
| A `OBJECT` | the ringed bullet, named, no question |
| **B `QUESTION`** | **selected** — the ringed bullet, "It was fired at a pope. Why is it in her crown?" |
| C `SEALED` | the sealed envelope, "Two popes read it." |

B is the selection. It is a photograph of the real object rather than a
reconstruction, and the promise on it is the promise the film keeps.

The bullet is ringed in ember. That is not decoration. Dull grey lead against
gold is invisible at thumbnail size, and a thumbnail that asserts something the
viewer cannot see is a thumbnail they scroll past. The ring sits where the
projectile actually is, at the junction of the arches beneath the sky-blue orb,
measured off the source frame.

The canvas is 1920x1080 rather than 4K because the only rights-clear photograph
of the crown is 1174x768. A larger canvas would be upscale with nothing in it.

C is the honest alternative to test against B, and the one to try if B
underperforms: it promises a document mystery instead of an object mystery.

## Tags

Front-loaded on the two queries with real volume, `fatima` and
`third secret of fatima`, then the people and places, then the format. 18 tags,
291 characters against the 500 limit.

## Interaction

The script asks for a one-word comment, `WORLD` or `MYSELF`, at the moment before
the document is opened, and asks again in the closing line. The pinned comment
repeats it. The question is not a generic call for engagement: it asks the viewer
to make the same reading decision the episode then examines, so their answer is
still live during the payoff.
