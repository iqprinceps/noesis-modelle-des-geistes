A bullet fired at Pope John Paul II in 1981 is set inside a crown in Portugal. This is how it got there.

Two months after he was shot, still recovering from surgery, he asked for an envelope that had been sealed for thirty-seven years. Two popes before him had opened it, read what was inside, and closed it again without explaining anything. The text is the part of the Fatima account commonly called the Third Secret, written out by Lucia dos Santos on the third of January, 1944.

This documentary follows the envelope rather than the rumour: from a girl with a pencil in 1944, to the archive of the Holy Office in 1957, through two popes who read and resealed it, to a hospital room on the eighteenth of July, 1981 — and then to what the page actually says, and what it never says.

The Vatican published the manuscript in 2000 as a photographic facsimile, so we can read exactly what he read. One page carried three readings at the same time: a century of martyrs, one afternoon in Rome, and one man's understanding of why he was still alive.

Which raises the question the episode is really about. When do we agree that a prophecy has been fulfilled? When it names an event in advance? Or when a person survives something and recognises their own experience inside an older text?

Before the page is opened, you get a choice. WORLD or MYSELF. Leave one word below — your answer changes how you read the rest.

ON ONE DATE
The Shrine confirms that the projectile is in the crown. The year it was set there, 1989, rests on consistent secondary reporting rather than on a statement from the Shrine or the Holy See, and the sources disagree about when the projectile was handed over. The episode therefore does not say who carried it, or where it was recovered. If a primary source turns up, this description will say so.
