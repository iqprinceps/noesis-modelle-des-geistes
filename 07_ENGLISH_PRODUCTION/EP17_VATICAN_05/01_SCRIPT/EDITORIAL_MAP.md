## Viewer promise

Two printed books from 1697 and 1698 describe the same young man and cannot agree
on what kind of reality they are describing. The finale asks how an institution
decides anything at all when the claimed cause leaves no trace, and closes the
five-file series.

## Structural change against the German master

Three defects, three fixes:

1. **A disclaimer sat inside the hook.** The German S1 stopped at roughly second
   25 to explain that the case is Protestant, English and polemical. The caveat
   is correct and it now arrives after the case has taken hold, reframed as the
   reason the case is useful rather than as an apology for using it.
2. **The case was never told.** The German version referenced a dispute whose
   underlying events the viewer never heard. The cold open now narrates what is
   claimed to have happened in the farmhouse, which is the promise the episode
   was already making.
3. **There was no scene and no person.** The 1999 criteria are now followed by a
   first-person decision in a room, with a family present, before the rite opens.

## Story movements

1. Lancashire, around 1689. The fits, the ministers, the claimed deliverance.
2. 1697 accusation, 1698 defence. Two books, one young man, two realities.
3. The reframe: this belongs to everybody, and Rome had been legislating it since 1614.
4. 1614 Rituale Romanum. The rite opens by instructing distrust of the diagnosis.
5. Canon 1172. Bishop, permission, qualification. Bureaucracy between claim and act.
6. 26 January 1999. Illness first, explicitly including psychological illness.
7. The famous signs, immediately placed inside a process rather than a test.
8. The decision in the room. Viewer decision.
9. Back to Surey: the events agreed, the category disputed.
10. The rite opened last: language as action, held in two readings at once.
11. Why the procedure survives.
12. Series payoff: envelope, archive, charter, list, rite.

## Interaction

Placed at movement 8, after the criteria and before the rite: `ILLNESS or
PRESENCE`. The script then names what the viewer actually did, which is choose an
explanation to act on under incomplete information. That line is the thesis of
the whole series and it only works because the viewer just performed it.

## Editorial guard

Never use epilepsy, psychosis, dissociation or trauma as possession texture, and
never use historical patient imagery as a visual synonym for possession. The
1999 threshold is a church criterion and is described as one. No claim is made
that any sign constitutes a diagnostic test.

## Primary visual anchors

- Taylor 1697 and Jollie 1698 title pages, Wellcome Public Domain Mark, as two
  opposed physical objects that recur throughout;
- the 1614 exorcism chapter opening as a real page;
- sober modern church interiors and ordinary rooms; candles only where they carry meaning;
- faces: ministers, physicians, the disputing parties, the affected person, handled
  with restraint and without horror grammar;
- closing return to all five series objects in their own materials.

## Source lock required before voice

**Hard gate.** The cold open narrates the Surey case, and the case details are
the least locked material in the package.

1. the young man's name, the year, the location and the nature of the fits,
   verified against the Taylor 1697 and Jollie 1698 scans rather than secondary
   summaries, with anything unverified removed from the narration;
2. the duration and character of the ministers' intervention, attributed to
   whichever of the two books asserts it;
3. 1614 printed p. 198 opening instruction, compared against the selected scan;
   the script paraphrases and no Latin appears in quotation marks until matched;
4. Canon 1172 wording;
5. the 1999 presentation wording in the chosen language edition, for both the
   permission requirement and the illness examination;
6. pronunciation sheet: Rituale Romanum, Jollie, Surey, ordinary.
