## What acquisition changed editorially

**EP17 gained its person.** Taylor 1697 prints a woodcut of Richard Dugdale
at the front of the book, captioned `The Surey Impostor.` It is the only
surviving portrait of him and it was made by the side that called him a
fraud. The episode previously had no human being in it. The cold open now
names him, gives him an occupation, and uses the partisan portrait as an
argument rather than as decoration.

The same volume carries a flyleaf note dated September 1894 in which a later
owner records the case and rules: `either a lunatic or an impostor; the
latter undoubtedly.` A third reader, two centuries on, picking the same
binary the episode puts to the viewer.

**EP15 gained a second forensic page.** The Coleman edition prints Latin and
English on facing pages. Printed pages 12 and 13 carry the forgery's own
words, `una cum omnibus satrapis nostris`, with the translation beside it.
Printed pages 86 and 87 carry Valla's attack. The viewer can now read the
evidence before the investigator does, which is what the hunt beat needed.

**EP13 closed its attack-footage gap without an agency licence.** The Fiat
Campagnola that John Paul II was riding in on 13 May 1981 is on Commons
under CC0 in its museum display, at 3210x2582. Object rather than newsreel,
which suits the series grammar better than footage would have.

## Open gaps

**EP14 has no hero photograph and no route to one.** The 81-seal letter is
the click reason and the thumbnail, and no rights-clear photograph of the
original was found. The rights lock specifies a schematic reconstruction. Of
the five episodes this is the only one whose central object cannot be shown.

**EP13 crown resolution.** 1174x768 is the only rights-clear photograph of
the object in existence as far as two searches establish. 1080p finish, slow
push, no macro. If the underlying Centro Televisivo Vaticano video carries
the same CC BY 3.0 licence, further frames could be extracted and the hero
object would gain movement. Worth one verification pass.

**EP17 Commons pool is unusable.** The inherited files include Ars Goetia
sigils and Dictionnaire Infernal demon plates. That is occult illustration,
and this episode is about institutional decision-making. Using them delivers
exactly the horror grammar the editorial guard forbids. The two Wellcome
volumes carry the episode instead.

**One file rejected on editorial grounds.** A Wellcome series of photographs
of a woman in a hysterical episode, CC BY 4.0. EP17 does not use historical
patient imagery as a visual synonym for possession. Not downloaded.

## A correction about the German research

An earlier pass in this session reported that roughly a third of the Commons
URLs in the German packages were dead. That was wrong, and the fault was in
the extraction, not the research. The URL pattern truncated every filename
containing brackets, which is most of the dated and disambiguated Commons
titles. Re-run with a corrected pattern: 148 titles, 147 resolve, and the one
failure is a CSV line-joining artifact rather than a missing file.

The asset research in `03_EPISODEN/TYPE_B` is sound.
