## Editorial verdict

The episode's historical spine is sound. The strongest version remains the current human-conflict structure: Descartes proposes a bodily mechanism and a privileged pineal role; Elisabeth presses the causal gap between an immaterial soul and an extended body. No generic rewrite is warranted.

Two precision changes are recommended and applied on this branch:

1. Name Elisabeth's second letter as **10 June 1643** instead of leaving it undated.
2. Replace the loose modern sentence `Consciousness now appears as a distributed problem rather than the function of one point` with wording that does not imply a settled single theory of consciousness: modern neuroscience investigates consciousness across distributed brain systems and does not treat the pineal gland as a single consciousness centre.

The script keeps **6 May 1643** as the editorial date for Elisabeth's first letter. Some historical catalogues/editions expose dual-calendar or editorial date variants; the Adam–Tannery reference is AT III 660–662 and modern scholarly references commonly cite 6 May 1643.

## Claim boundaries

- `principal seat of the soul`, pineal motion and animal-spirit flow are always attributed to Descartes' historical model.
- Do not imply Descartes discovered the pineal gland.
- Do not imply Descartes taught a modern occult `third eye` doctrine.
- Do not say modern neuroscience has solved the mind–body problem or found one seat of consciousness.
- Elisabeth's argument should remain a historically grounded philosophical objection, not a claim that she experimentally disproved Descartes.
