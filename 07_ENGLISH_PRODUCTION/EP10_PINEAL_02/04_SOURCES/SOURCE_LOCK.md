## Editorial verdict

The episode's historical spine remains sound. The strongest version is the current human-conflict structure: Descartes proposes a bodily mechanism and a privileged pineal role; Elisabeth presses the causal gap between an immaterial soul and an extended body. No generic rewrite is warranted.

Two precision changes were already applied on this branch:

1. Name Elisabeth's second letter as **10 June 1643** instead of leaving it undated.
2. Use a modern-neuroscience boundary that does not imply a settled single theory of consciousness or a pineal consciousness centre.

## Date convention and facsimile warning

The English narration uses the **manuscript-date convention** `6 May 1643` and `10 June 1643` for Elisabeth's two key letters. Adam–Tannery/Wikisource navigation can expose dual-calendar/editorial variants such as **16 May** and **20 June**. Production must preserve the source label in provenance and must never retouch a facsimile to hide the difference.

The critical point is consistency: narration uses the manuscript-date convention; source metadata records exactly what the acquired edition/catalogue says.
