### Date convention

The English narration uses the manuscript-date convention **6 May** and **10 June 1643**. Adam–Tannery/Wikisource navigation can expose dual-calendar/editorial variants such as **16 May** and **20 June**. Do not alter source images to conceal this; record the exact source label in metadata while keeping narration internally consistent.

## Rights / provenance rules

- Prefer original historical portraits, books, diagrams and documents wherever they exist.
- Generated images may reconstruct rooms, hands, movement, the eye experiment and transitions, but never substitute for a named person, historical page or scientific source.
- Every binary gets: source landing URL, resolved download URL, acquisition timestamp, institution/creator, date, rights statement, licence URL where relevant, MIME type, dimensions/page count, byte size, SHA-256 and local filename.
- `CC BY` assets require exact attribution in credits/provenance. `CC0`/PD assets still retain source/institution credit in the production manifest.
- Large binaries stay in external production storage, not GitHub.
