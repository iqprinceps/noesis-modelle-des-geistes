# EP09_EN — Claim Map

## Established story anchors

- Several non-mammalian vertebrates possess parietal/pineal photoreceptive systems; some species have a developed parietal eye.
- Retinal and pineal tissues have deep evolutionary and molecular relationships.
- The human pineal gland is not directly light-sensitive like a parietal eye.
- Human light timing reaches the pineal gland through a retinal, circadian and neural pathway.
- Melatonin functions as a biological night/timing signal.
- Under controlled study conditions, evening exposure to a light-emitting reader suppressed the evening melatonin rise, delayed circadian timing and affected subsequent sleepiness/alertness.

## Attribution and open meaning

- Species-specific anatomy and function remain tied to zoological sources.
- Evolution is described as a branching relationship, never as the simple claim that a reptile eye became the human pineal gland.
- `Third eye` at the end is a viewer interpretation question, not a claim of hidden human vision.
