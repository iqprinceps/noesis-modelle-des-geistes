# EP04A_EN Editorial Map

## Promise

Jung warned Westerners about Kundalini after surviving an inner landscape with a
serpent of his own. Projection and recurring structure remain in competition.

## Eight-act map

1. **The warning and two serpents** — source, contradiction, unresolved theory fork.
2. **The flood** — Freud rupture, train vision, fear of psychosis, later reinterpretation.
3. **Entering deliberately** — active imagination, cave, serpent, Philemon.
4. **The ground** — work and family hold the inner world in tension.
5. **Zurich 1932** — Hauer, the seminar, and Jung's psychological translation.
6. **The moving map** — six centers, Sahasrara, and the later familiar chart.
7. **Possession by affect** — a human scene makes Jung's reading tangible.
8. **Map or territory** — three theories, viewer decision, Pauli handoff.

## First-minute anchors

- 0–8 s: Jung's warning inside authentic 1932 context.
- 8–16 s: water, cave, red sun, black serpent as subjective imagery.
- 16–25 s: historical Kundalini diagram remains physically separate.
- 25–35 s: resemblance shown without fusing the symbols.
- 35–48 s: train landscape begins to flood.
- 48–60 s: clinical reality and Jung's fear of psychosis.

## Claim locks

- Jung's 1913 serpent is an open parallel, not identified as Kundalini.
- The flood is a vision Jung reinterpreted after the war began.
- Chakra imagery is historically variable and culturally situated.
- Abstract imagery is subjective, never fake archive.

## Interaction and handoff

- Central theory fork: projection, archetype, or recognition.
- Viewer decision: `MAP` or `MIRROR`.
- Handoff: Pauli enters and coincidence begins to answer back.
