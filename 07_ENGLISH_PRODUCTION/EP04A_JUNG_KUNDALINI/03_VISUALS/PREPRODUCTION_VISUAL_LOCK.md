# EP04A Jung and Kundalini — Preproduction Visual Lock

## Viewer diagnosis of the German material

This is the best visual baseline of the four: high variety, no significant asset
reuse, quieter cards, and a more cinematic relationship between archive and
reconstruction. The remaining weakness is tonal. Several cards turn a dangerous
inner journey into a lesson or self-help instruction, while the source genealogy
briefly becomes classroom history.

The English edition should preserve the restraint and make the map itself unstable.

## Keep / rework / retire

- the restrained serpent, cave, water, red sun, and train visual motifs;
- the sparse `MAP / MIRROR` idea and Pauli handoff;

### RETIRE

- generic wellness chakra wheels and rainbow-body stock language;
- cards that instruct the viewer how to regulate emotion;
- a flat `SUBSCRIBE` button;
- any literal depiction proving visions were prophetic or externally real;
- generated manuscripts or books containing fake readable text.

## Visual story

Two maps approach each other from different times: Jung's 1913 inner landscape and
the Indian/Western textual map in front of him in 1932. The tension comes from their
resemblance and their non-equivalence. The viewer must decide whether Jung found a
recurring human structure or projected himself into another tradition.

## Abstract / mystical layer

This episode receives the richest subjective imagery of the series: the train
landscape drowning in red water, descent past a submerged sun, figures responding
without Jung's command, the serpent crossing between darkness and manuscript
linework, and Philemon occupying space like an independent intelligence. The inner
world may be beautiful and frightening; the return to daylight, family, and clinical
work keeps the viewer oriented.
