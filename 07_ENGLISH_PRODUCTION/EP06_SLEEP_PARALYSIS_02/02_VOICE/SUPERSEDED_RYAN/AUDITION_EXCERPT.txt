In 1692, Richard Coman told a Salem court about a night no one else could see. He said the house was locked. A light was burning. His wife lay beside him. Then Bridget Bishop entered the room. Coman said she lay across his chest and pressed him down so completely that he could neither speak nor move—not even enough to wake his wife. Years after the night itself, he put that memory under oath. Eight days after his testimony, Bridget Bishop was hanged. The record cannot tell us what state produced Coman's night. It tells us what Salem did with it.
