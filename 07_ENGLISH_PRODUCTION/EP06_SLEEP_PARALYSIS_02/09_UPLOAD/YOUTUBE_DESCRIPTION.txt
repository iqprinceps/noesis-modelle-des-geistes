A locked room. A crushing weight on the chest. A witness unable to speak or move.

In 1692, Richard Coman described that experience to a Salem court. His account entered the case against Bridget Bishop. Eight days after his testimony, she was hanged.

This sleep paralysis documentary follows one unsettling question across court records, folklore, art and modern research: when the body produces paralysis, pressure and a sensed presence, can the story waiting for that experience change what happens next?

From Henry Fuseli's The Nightmare to David Hufford's Old Hag research, and from Salem to sleep-paralysis samples in Egypt and Denmark, a recurring bodily pattern acquires very different faces. Science enters as a second lens—not to erase the experience, but to explore how expectation, fear and sleep can become entangled.
