## Description

In 1692, Richard Coman told a Salem court that Bridget Bishop entered his
locked room, pressed down on his chest, and left him unable to speak or move.
Eight days after his testimony, Bishop was hanged.

This episode follows one unsettling question across court records, folklore,
art and sleep research: when the body produces paralysis, pressure and a sensed
presence, can the story waiting for that experience change what happens next?

From Henry Fuseli's *The Nightmare* to the Old Hag tradition studied by David
Hufford, and from Salem to research samples in Egypt and Denmark, the same
bodily structure acquires very different faces. Science enters here as a
counterpoint—not to erase the experience, but to ask how expectation, fear and
sleep can become entangled.
