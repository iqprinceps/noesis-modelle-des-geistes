# EP12_EN — Production Plan

## Working title

**DMT at the Threshold**

## Editorial engine

A laboratory mystery: a famous pineal-DMT-at-death story is placed under experimental pressure. The strongest reveal is not that DMT disappears when the pineal gland is removed, but that it remains measurable in rat visual cortex and can rise after experimentally induced cardiac arrest under both conditions.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. **Primary documentary evidence:** Dean et al. 2019 full paper, Figure 1, Figure 4.
2. **Human phenomenology evidence:** Timmermann et al. 2018 full paper / Table 1.
3. **Attributed cultural hypothesis:** Rick Strassman context, only after exact edition/page and reproduction rights are checked if quoted/shown.
4. **Generated reconstruction:** laboratory procedure, microdialysis, analytical measurement, human-measurement thought experiment, subjective threshold imagery.

Archive/scientific evidence and generated reconstruction must never be confused in metadata or edit notes.

## Document reveal rules

- Show the complete relevant page/figure first at readable scale.
- Then crop/zoom only the exact data being discussed.
- Keep figure labels and source attribution legible in at least one shot.
- Never recreate the scientific chart as if it were the original.
- Generated diagrams may simplify method, but should be labeled internally as reconstruction in the cue sheet/manifests.

## Audio / rhythm notes

- Cold open: sparse low-frequency room tone, no mystical choir.
- First Dean-paper reveal: reduce music; let paper/monitor sounds carry authority.
- Pinealectomy reveal: one clean sonic pivot, not a trailer hit.
- Cardiac-arrest result: tension from pulse/room-tone interruption, avoiding literal flatline cliché if it distracts from the rat study.
- Human NDE section: widen space and texture, but keep voices/intelligibility primary.
- Final series payoff: return to the motif used across the pineal series, then remove it before the last question.

## Production safeguards

- No animal-surgery spectacle.
- No literal visual of a soul leaving the body.
- No psychedelic neon wallpaper behind scientific claims.
- No human cardiac-arrest footage presented as if it were the Dean experiment.
- No claim that a human terminal DMT surge has been measured.
- No implication that phenomenological overlap proves a common biochemical cause.
