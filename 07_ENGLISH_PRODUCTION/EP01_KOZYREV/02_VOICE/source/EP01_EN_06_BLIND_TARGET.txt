Imagine four sealed target images.

A red lighthouse surrounded by ice.

A violin on blue fabric.

A burning car in an empty field.

A white horse standing in water.

Which target would you choose to hide?

Hold it in your mind.

Only after the participant enters the chamber does a computer randomly select one image.

Nobody present knows which one.

The participant speaks or draws whatever appears.

Independent judges compare the description with all four targets using rules fixed before the session.

Then another laboratory repeats the procedure.

If the hidden image is the lighthouse and the participant says tower, red light, ice, that is interesting.

But one resemblance can be found by chance.

Or created by interpretation after the answer is known.

Repeated identification under blinded conditions would be different.

It would force all three theories apart.

Isolation could explain an intense state.

A physical change could explain a measurable bodily effect.

Neither should reveal a randomly selected target.
