# EP01 Kozyrev — Preproduction Visual Lock

## Viewer-label override — binding for EP01

The visual-mode terms `ARCHIVE`, `ORIGINAL DOCUMENT`, `RECONSTRUCTION`,
`MODEL`, and `INNER / HYPOTHESIS` are internal metadata only. They must not be
burned into the viewer image as permanent badges.

- Abstract and mystical images carry no visible production-category label.
- Reconstructions are distinguished by a consistent contemporary cinematic
  language. A subtle 1.5–2.0 second contextual line is optional only at the
  first entrance into one continuous reconstruction block; it then disappears.
- A primary document may receive one concise source/date line or an original-line
  highlight at its first relevant reveal. It does not receive a mode badge.
- Authentic portraits carry no archive badge. Source and rights information live
  in the asset manifest and video credits.
- `RECONSTRUCTION`, `MODEL`, and `INNER / HYPOTHESIS` remain required internal
  fields in cue, manifest, and QA records so editorial status is never lost.

This episode-specific viewer-label override supersedes contradictory earlier
series rules while preserving their evidence-boundary intent.

## Quantity and production-order override — binding for EP01

All previously named quantities are candidate scopes, never creative quotas.
The nine motion concepts, archive crops, stills, cards, maps, and reserve images
may be merged, reduced, or omitted after voice alignment. An asset is generated
only when the aligned cue sheet demonstrates a viewer-facing need that cannot be
served better by a licensed source, an accepted existing state, or a restrained
edit move.

Production order is locked to:

1. source/right review and canonical script/hook audit;
2. two short, identical-text voice auditions;
3. one selected full master plus targeted pickups;
4. forced alignment;
5. time-based cue sheet;
6. only the assets required by that aligned cue sheet.

There will not be two complete voice versions. The early patent reveal remains
an episode-specific hook decision, not a universal fifteen-second source quota.

## Viewer diagnosis of the German material

The object is inherently compelling, but the existing edit spends its advantage
too early. Multiple chamber images repeat before their meaning changes. The cards
are accurate and carefully designed, yet they read like an editorial dashboard:
thin lines, small labels, several facts per frame, and little human tension.

The viewer understands more, but does not continually need the next shot. This is
the episode requiring the largest visual rebuild.

## Keep / rework / retire

### KEEP

- authentic Kozyrev portrait and dated biographical archive;
- original patent cover, drawings, title, dates, inventors, and dimensions;
- strongest full-scale chamber reference with credible human scale;
- any language-neutral technical image that clearly shows panel curvature.

### REWORK

- patent diagrams become animated assembly and rotation states;
- source cards become close document crops with one English translation line;
- chamber references guide new coherent reconstructions with consistent geometry;
- the patent timeline becomes a physical contradiction: `1983` then `1996`.

### RETIRE

- all seven dense blue dashboard cards;
- repeated seated-person chamber images with no new story function;
- generic glowing auras, laser-like energy beams, and meditation clichés;
- a generic `YES / NO` interaction card;
- any image implying that remote perception or time access occurred.

## Visual story

The spiral changes meaning five times: impossible object, engineered machine,
claimed field device, controlled test chamber, and unresolved empty room. Its
lighting becomes less mystical as the evidence becomes more precise.

## Abstract / mystical layer

The chamber should feel capable of bending perception before the narration tests
that possibility. Use delayed reflections, conflicting motion directions, lunar
shadows moving across polished metal, a distant landscape fragmented inside the
spiral, and an empty chamber whose shadow continues to turn. These are subjective
metaphors, not literal demonstrations of an effect.
