# EP03_EN Editorial Map

## Promise

One unnamed operator may account for about half of a twenty-eight-year Princeton
anomaly. The viewer must decide when that should have closed the laboratory.

## Eight-act map

1. **Operator 10** — promise the anonymous person before explaining the machine.
2. **The dean and the financier** — Jahn, McDonnell, Princeton's conditions.
3. **The machines** — random-event generator and mechanical cascade.
4. **One bit in ten thousand** — more data may reveal a signal or amplify method.
5. **The identity gap** — fifteen percent of trials, roughly half the excess.
6. **Three laboratories** — fixed rules, expected result, failed replication.
7. **The story refuses to end** — off-time trials and the baseline bind.
8. **Closure** — scientific claim resolves; the human model of intention stays open.

## First-minute anchors

- 0–7 s: fourteen million points compress around one anonymous absence.
- 7–14 s: `about half the excess`; identity stays hidden.
- 14–22 s: gray machine and one-bit-in-ten-thousand animation.
- 22–32 s: authentic Jahn identity and twenty-eight-year cost.
- 32–45 s: basement and Princeton context.
- 45–60 s: McDonnell, aircraft, and the subjective-radiation fear.

## Precision locks

- `One bit in ten thousand` is an aggregate effect size, not a successful event.
- Operator 10 remains unidentified.
- Princeton permitting the lab is not Princeton endorsing its claims.
- The replication failure is stated before later reinterpretations.

## Interaction and handoff

- Central decision: `OPERATOR 10`, `REPLICATION`, or `NOT YET`.
- Final theory fork: signal, flaw, or intention before conscious ownership.
- Handoff: the machine disappears; Jung confronts apparently autonomous images.
