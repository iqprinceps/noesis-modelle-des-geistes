## Viewer promise

Eighty-three of the most powerful men in England hung eighty-one seals from one
sheet of parchment to force a pope's hand. They lost. The letter survived anyway,
while cartloads of the same archive were sold as waste paper. The episode is
about which parts of the past are still here to argue about.

## Structural change against the German master

The German version answered the survival question thematically and withheld
nothing, which left the episode without a reveal. Two changes:

1. **The loss is the reveal.** Documents sold by weight during the return from
   Paris is the shock, and it is planted in the cold open as a promise and paid
   at the midpoint. It is not a fourth item in a list.
2. **Galileo is cut entirely.** The German S7 announced its own deferral to EP16
   on screen and paid nothing. Its runtime goes to the Napoleon consequence and
   the bunker as the institutional answer to 1810.

## Story movements

1. The sheet, the signers, the demand, the refusal. The survival, and the waste paper.
2. Secretum as a translation problem; an archive decides what can still be proved.
3. Access since 1881; the real barrier is knowing what to ask for.
4. 1810, roughly 3,239 crates over the Alps. Viewer decision at the crates.
5. The return: sorted, destroyed, sold by weight, lost. No conspiracy, just budgets.
6. Back to the seals, now as a survivor rather than a curiosity.
7. The 1980 bunker as the answer to 1810.
8. Digitisation: preserved, catalogued, and still unseen.
9. Series form named, then the forged charter handoff.

## Interaction

Placed at movement 4, at the exact historical decision: `KEEP or LEAVE`. The
viewer allocates a budget they do not have, and movement 5 then reports what was
actually chosen. Repeated in the final line.
