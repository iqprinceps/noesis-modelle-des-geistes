# EP14_EN — Visual plan

94 beats. The episode is about what survives and what does not, so the picture
has to be able to show both, and to tell them apart at a glance.

## Three registers

EP13 used two, HISTORICAL and VISION. A vision register would be wrong here:
nothing in this episode is imagined. What this one needs instead is a way to show
absence, because roughly a third of it is about material that no longer exists.

**A · HISTORICAL.** Naturalistic photography and faithful reproduction of real
things: portraits, archive rooms, the Cortile, period objects. Available light,
real materials, full tonal range. Genuine photographs and paintings live here.

**B · DOCUMENT.** The objects themselves, shot the way a conservator would: even
raking light across a flat surface, shallow depth, colour accurate, the material
rendered as material. Parchment fibre, wax, tin, silk cord, iron gall ink. This
register carries the 1530 letter and every seal.

**C · LOSS.** What is gone. Cool, low contrast, wide, under-populated: empty
shelving where boxes stood, dust outlines, a cart on a road, scales. Never
theatrical, never ruined-castle gothic. The register's job is to make absence
legible without dramatising it, because the episode's point is that most of the
past disappears for dull reasons.

Registers must be visually separable within a second. A viewer should never have
to ask whether they are looking at a thing that exists.

## The object does not exist as a free image

The 1530 letter is Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, A.A., Arm. I-XVIII, 4098A. It
survives only as a commercial facsimile edition published with the archive; there
is no openly licensed photograph of it anywhere. It is the object the whole
episode is built on, so it has to be reconstructed.

That reconstruction is built from a documented description rather than invented:

| | |
|---|---|
| Sheet | parchment, 91.5 x 46 cm |
| Signatures | 83, in 13 columns |
| Seals | 81, each in its own tin skippet |
| Empty positions | 4 |
| Attachment | red silk cord through the fold |
| Date | 13 July 1530 |
| Signatories | 2 archbishops, 2 dukes, 2 marquesses, 13 earls, 4 bishops, 1 viscount, 26 barons, 22 abbots |

Five views are needed, because the object carries the episode:

1. the whole sheet from above, columns visible, seals hanging below the frame
2. the row of skippets from the side, in a line, the cord under tension
3. one skippet in macro, open, the wax impression inside
4. one of the four empty positions, cord and no case
5. the signature columns close, ink and hand visible but no readable name

Every one of these is labelled a reconstruction in the rights register, and the
description says so. No signature is legible, because inventing the autograph of
a named historical person is exactly what this channel does not do.

## What the existing pool actually gives

71 files were inherited. 18 belong to an earlier, wider draft on Vatican secrets
and have nothing to do with this script: Baphomet, the Templars, Galileo. 10 more
carry a ShareAlike condition and cannot be used at all, and unfortunately that
includes every seal photograph in the pool.

43 are usable, and they are strongest exactly where the episode needs faces:
Henry VIII, Catherine of Aragon, Clement VII, Anne Boleyn, Wolsey, plus four
views of the Cortile della Pigna and two archive interiors.

## What research added

Leo XIII in very large Library of Congress plates, Pius XII including 1958, a
wax seal in a tin box from Kloster Isenhagen, AAV register scans, Lorenzo Valla
portraits and two paintings of the Donation of Constantine for the handoff, and
the Hotel de Soubise, which is where the archive physically went in 1810.

## What has to be generated

Everything in the LOSS register, the whole 1530 object, the Napoleon transport,
and the working life of an archive: hands on a register, a reading room, a
call slip, a catalogue drawer, a digitisation rig. Roughly sixty states.

## Two corrections the research produced

Of 3,239 crates taken to Paris, about 2,450 came back. And the parchments that
did not were not simply "sold as waste paper": they were sold by weight to a
Paris grocer, for packing. Both are better than what the script currently says
and both are in the source lock.

## Rules carried over from EP13, and why

- **No ShareAlike anywhere.** The Ken Burns pass makes the film Adapted Material.
- **Named historical figures appear only in real portraits or photographs.**
  Nothing generated ever stands in for Henry VIII, Clement VII or Napoleon.
- **No blank paper.** Every writing surface carries ink that reads as writing and
  resolves into no readable word. A blank sheet reads as a placeholder.
- **People have faces.** Crowds, clerks, carters and researchers are ordinary
  unnamed people, seen and lit.
- **Documents hold still.** Fine hands and engraved detail shimmer when moved a
  fraction of a pixel per frame, so they are locked frames.
