Researchers interrupted sleep in sixteen healthy people. Six isolated sleep-paralysis episodes followed: one, two, three, four, five, six.

They slept, were awakened at selected times, stayed awake for an hour, then returned to bed under polysomnography.

Across four nights, the episodes appeared under that protocol, but not after every interruption or in every participant.

When they occurred, participants knew they were in the lab and could not move. All but one also reported disturbing visual or auditory experiences.

The recordings caught the contradiction: waking awareness while muscle atonia persisted.

The doorway was measurable. Why did the room sometimes feel occupied?
