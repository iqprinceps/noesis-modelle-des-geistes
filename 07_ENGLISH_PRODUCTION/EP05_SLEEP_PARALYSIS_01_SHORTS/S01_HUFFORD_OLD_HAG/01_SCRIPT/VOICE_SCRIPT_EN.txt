The door opens. Footsteps cross the room. You try to move—nothing. Then the mattress sinks beside you.

This happened to David Hufford before he became a folklorist.

Years later, in Newfoundland, strangers described the same sequence and gave the visitor a name he said he had never heard: the Old Hag.

REM physiology can explain why awareness returns while the muscles are still held quiet. But Hufford's puzzle begins after that: why the footsteps, the weight, the certainty of intention—and why the same shape in other lives?

Does folklore teach us what to meet? Or do recurring encounters teach folklore what to remember?
