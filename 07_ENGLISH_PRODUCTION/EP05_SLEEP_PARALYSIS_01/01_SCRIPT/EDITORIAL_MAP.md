# EP05_EN Editorial Map — Sleep Paralysis I

## Viewer promise

David Hufford encountered the Old Hag pattern before he knew its name. The film
will show what a sleep laboratory can explain, then stay with the stranger
question: why does paralysis so often feel like another awareness in the room?

## Hook choice

The canonical opening uses a real person and a contradiction, not an anonymous
sleep-paralysis scenario. Within the first movement the viewer receives:

1. Hufford's name and the 1963 encounter;
2. the approaching steps, immobility, sinking mattress and chest pressure;
3. the later Newfoundland reports and unknown Old Hag name;
4. the promise that the state can be measured while the presence remains the
   episode's unresolved center.

## Retention loops

- Open: how could Hufford experience a pattern he did not know?
- Partial payoff: REM atonia explains why movement fails.
- New loop: why do footsteps, pressure and an agent appear?
- Laboratory loop/payoff: can the state be made more likely and measured?
- Participation: which arrives first — pressure, sound or presence?
- Mystery peak: some people feel an awareness before any figure exists.
- Callback: experience and folklore may form a feedback loop.
- Series handoff: a similar private night becomes courtroom evidence in Salem.

## Interaction design

The central prompt is `PRESSURE / SOUND / PRESENCE`. It supports a one-word
answer while naturally inviting people with lived experience to describe the
order of sensations. The subscribe prompt appears only after the Bridget Bishop
promise has landed.

## Tone

- intimate, nocturnal and precise;
- science as a door into the mystery, not a dismissive verdict;
- theories remain grammatically open without timid or belittling language;
- no permanent reconstruction or hypothesis labels;
- no generic horror exaggeration and no classroom-style summary cards.
