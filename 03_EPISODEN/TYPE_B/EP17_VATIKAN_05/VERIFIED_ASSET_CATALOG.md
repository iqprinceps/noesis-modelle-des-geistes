# EP17 VATICAN V — Verified Asset Catalog

**Episode:** *The Ritual Against the Unseen*  
**Research/URL verification date:** 2026-08-30  
**Purpose:** production-facing list of original evidence, historical scans, portraits, medical-history material, art and clearly labelled occult contrast material.  

## Status legend

- **GREEN — production-friendly:** source page verified and an open/public-domain licence is explicitly stated or the file is clearly marked public domain/CC0/CC BY.
- **AMBER — evidence/reference:** URL and content verified, but no open reuse licence is stated on the source page. Use for fact-checking; screenshot/reproduction in the finished film needs rights/legal review.
- **HOLD — lead only:** real and relevant collection item, but not currently an online/open production asset.

## Editorial rule

Historical art, occult books and medical photographs are **contextual illustrations**, not photographs of a verified supernatural event. Medical material is **not** evidence against a theological claim, and occult/demonological material is **not** to be presented as Catholic or Vatican liturgy.

### Historical text note

The 1614 book already contains a chapter instructing the exorcist not to believe too readily that someone is possessed and to distinguish signs from disease. Do **not** retroject the modern wording “especially psychological illness” into 1614: that modern formulation is documented in the later rite/Catechism. The episode may safely say that caution and differentiation were already built into the older ritual tradition.

# J. Rights / attribution cheat sheet

- **Vatican / Holy See pages:** factually authoritative, but no open image-reuse licence was found on the page. Treat screenshots as **rights-review required**.
- **Wellcome Public Domain Mark items:** credit the title + “Source: Wellcome Collection.”
- **Wellcome CC BY item D06:** credit Wellcome Collection/Wellcome Library as specified, link CC BY 4.0 and indicate edits/crops.
- **Wikimedia PD/CC0 files:** retain the exact file-page attribution and original museum/institution where supplied.
- **Library of Congress G01/G04:** rights statement says public domain/free to use and reuse; credit **Library of Congress**.
- **Google Books historical scans:** underlying books are public-domain-age works, but use the Google copy as evidence/search unless the scan's reuse terms are separately cleared. Prefer the Commons/Wellcome/Princeton versions for actual frames.

# K. False-association guardrails

1. Never label *Dictionnaire infernal*, Ars Goetia sigils or folklore prints as “the Vatican's exorcism book.”
2. Never label a Salpêtrière photograph as “a possessed woman.” Use the historical catalogue terminology and contextualise it as medical history.
3. Never use a stock-horror face as documentary evidence.
4. Do not imply that the four 1999 signs are a laboratory test; the Vatican presentation calls them criteria/indications used toward a **prudent** judgment.
5. Do not imply that the 1614 text used the exact modern phrase “psychological illness.”
6. Do not imply that modern psychiatry has a single explanation for every religious possession claim; describe clinical overlap and culturally framed dissociative phenomena precisely.
7. When using historical art, put artist/title/date on first appearance or in the source slate.
