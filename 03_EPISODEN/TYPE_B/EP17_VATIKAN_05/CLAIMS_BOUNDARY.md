# EP17 — Claims Boundary

**Verified:** 2026-08-30

| Claim | Allowed | Boundary / correction |
|---|---|---|
| Canon 1172 | Solemn/major exorcism over a possessed person requires the special and express permission of the local ordinary | Do not imply every priest may spontaneously perform it. |
| Exorcist | Permission is given to a priest distinguished by piety, knowledge, prudence and integrity of life | No invented “Vatican exorcist licence.” |
| 1999 | Revised rite officially presented/promulgated in 1999 after roughly ten years of work | Do not imply exorcism was invented in 1999. |
| 1999 criteria | Unknown languages, hidden/distant knowledge, disproportionate strength and violent aversion to the sacred are listed as signs/criteria considered toward a prudent judgment | Never present these as scientifically validated tests or automatic proof of possession. |
| Medicine | Modern church texts explicitly require careful differentiation from illness, especially psychological illness | No remote diagnosis of real people; no claim that medicine can or cannot settle a theological ontology. |
| 1614 | The Paul V *Rituale Romanum* contains `De exorcizandis obsessis a daemonio`; editio princeps exorcism chapter begins p. 198 | Do not quote the modern phrase “especially psychological illness” as if it were verbatim 1614 language. Older ritual cautions against easy credulity/disease confusion can be described separately. |
| 1623 scan | A full PD/No-Known-Copyright historical edition is available through Princeton/Wikimedia | Do not call a 1623 page the literal 1614 first-edition page. Caption the edition correctly. |
| Medical history | Charcot/Salpêtrière and Janet are valid history-of-neurology/psychology/dissociation context | Do not call Charcot or Janet “researchers who proved/disproved possession”; do not relabel historical patients as possessed. |
| Modern psychiatry | ICD-11 and clinical literature recognise culturally framed dissociative/possession-trance phenomena and stress cultural context | Do not collapse all religious possession beliefs into a psychiatric diagnosis. Accepted collective cultural/religious practice is not equivalent to the pathological diagnostic category. |
| Historical art | Christian art can show how exorcism/demons were represented across centuries | It is art, not documentary evidence of a supernatural event. |
| Occult imagery | *Dictionnaire infernal* / Ars Goetia can illustrate later demonological/esoteric imagination | **Never** imply these books, demon portraits or sigils belong to the Roman Ritual or Vatican liturgy. |

## Narrative boundary

The episode does not decide whether demonic possession objectively exists. It documents that the Catholic Church has a real, regulated ritual and an institutional procedure for cases it interprets in that theological category, while modern clinical frameworks describe overlapping experiences in different terms.

## No sensational claims

- Do not identify an ill or distressed real person as “possessed” without an exceptionally strong, consented and contextually appropriate source basis.
- No viral exorcism videos as proof.
- No invented Vatican case files.
- No fake manuscript/document graphics presented as originals.
- No demonological engraving or occult sigil presented as Catholic evidence.
- No patient image used as horror texture without historical/medical context.
