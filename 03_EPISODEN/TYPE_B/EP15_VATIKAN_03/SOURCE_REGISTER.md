## Gratian / canon-law correction

The previous shorthand “the Donation landed in Gratian’s Decretum” is too loose for a forensic episode. The safer formulation is:

> **The Donation entered the later textual/canon-law tradition of Gratian’s Decretum; the relevant passages are the Paleae in D.96 c.13–14. Scholarship distinguishes these additions from Gratian’s core compilation.**

Useful edition reference: Friedberg, *Corpus Iuris Canonici*, vol. I, D.96 cc.13–14, around pp./cols.342–345. Fried 2007 discusses/reproduces the Gratian/Friedberg material in the appendices (around pp.148–151 of the PDF/book pagination shown there).

This distinction should be reflected in narration and any on-screen label.

## Print-history correction

Do **not** call Hutten’s 1517 publication the first printed edition. Bibliographic scholarship records a **Strasbourg 1506 editio princeps**. Hutten’s later printing remains important for circulation/reception and can still be used dramatically, but the script/card must not label it “first edition.”

## Claims discipline

1. Never say “the Vatican forged the Donation.” Authorship/origin is not established that way.
2. Never present a medieval copy as Constantine’s original document.
3. Never imply all papal temporal power depended on this one text.
4. Do not present Raphael/San Silvestro imagery as evidence that the depicted event occurred; it is evidence of the legend’s iconographic life.
5. Valla was a devastating critic, not necessarily the first person ever to doubt the Donation.
6. “Forged” is historically useful shorthand, but the exact origin, author and first purpose remain debated; the episode should preserve that uncertainty.
7. Every visible text highlight must be tied to the exact scan/edition page in the production manifest.
