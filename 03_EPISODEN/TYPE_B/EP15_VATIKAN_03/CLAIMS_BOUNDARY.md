# EP15 — Claims Boundary

**Verification update:** 2026-08-30.

## Hard claims

| Claim | Safe formulation | Boundary / evidence lock |
|---|---|---|
| Donation | Text claims to speak for Constantine and grants Sylvester/Roman church privileges, imperial honors and territorial authority | Never present a medieval copy as an authentic 4th-c imperial diploma |
| Dating | Scholarship generally places the Constitutum/Donation in the 8th century | Do not invent exact author, scriptorium or date; origin/function remain debated |
| Use | The text was copied and used in medieval arguments about papal authority | Never claim all papal temporal power rested on this document alone |
| Gratian tradition | Donation material entered the later textual/canon-law tradition surrounding Gratian’s *Decretum* | Relevant material is conventionally cited as **Paleae, D.96 c.13–14**; do not imply Gratian himself simply inserted it in his original core compilation |
| Valla | Lorenzo Valla argued around 1440 that the Donation was not authentic | Do not call him the first person ever to doubt it |
| Method | Valla used historical, linguistic and institutional contradictions/anachronisms | On-screen examples only from locked pages/edition: Coleman p.87, pp.93–97, pp.125–127, p.163 |
| Baptism | Donation/Sylvester tradition has Sylvester baptize Constantine in Rome; Eusebius places Constantine’s baptism at the end of his life near Nicomedia | Standard accounts identify Eusebius of Nicomedia as baptizing bishop; keep primary-source vs later-legend distinction visible |
| Print | Valla’s treatise circulated in manuscript and was printed after his death | Current bibliographic first-print lock: **Strasbourg 1506**; do not call Hutten 1517/1518 the first edition |
| Vat. lat. 5314 | Vatican Library holds/digitizes a manuscript of Valla’s work | Professional film/TV/multimedia image use requires BAV permission; do not equate online access with free reuse |
| Raphael/Donation imagery | Later art shows how the Donation story was visualized and monumentalized | Never use later art as proof that the depicted transfer historically happened |

## Exact evidence anchors

- Donation territorial grant: Coleman 1922 translation, printed pp.17–19.
- Valla “satraps”: p.87.
- Valla Constantinople chronology/institutional critique: pp.93–97.
- Valla western grant wording: pp.125–127.
- Valla conclusion: p.163.
- Baptism: Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* IV.61–62.
- Gratian tradition: D.96 Paleae cc.13–14; Friedberg vol. I around 342–345; modern discussion/appendix in Johannes Fried 2007.

## Disallowed overstatements

- “The Vatican forged the document.”
- “Without the forgery, the Papal States would not have existed.”
- “Valla destroyed papal power with one sentence.”
- “The Church knew for centuries that it was false.”
- “Gratian himself inserted the Donation into the Decretum” without manuscript/editorial qualification.
- “Hutten first printed Valla in 1517” as an unqualified first-edition claim.
- Any modern conspiracy claim presented as established historical fact.

## Visual evidence boundary

- **St. Gallen Cod. Sang. 670 p.318:** evidence of medieval textual transmission, not Constantine’s original.
- **Vat. lat. 5314:** evidence of Valla manuscript transmission; rights-sensitive BAV reproduction.
- **Bartoli/Raphael / San Silvestro Donation scenes:** evidence of iconographic reception.
- **Constantine coins/medallions/bronze:** material culture of Constantine’s era; do not imply direct connection to the Donation.
- **Valla autograph/marginalia:** evidence of Valla’s textual/humanist working world; not necessarily pages from the Donation treatise unless specifically identified.

## Mystique without self-refutation

The episode can let the enormous claim work emotionally before the forensic turn. The reveal does not make the story smaller: the historically false origin becomes more interesting because it acquired real documentary, legal and visual authority. The payoff remains:

> A document does not need to describe a real event to become a real historical force.
