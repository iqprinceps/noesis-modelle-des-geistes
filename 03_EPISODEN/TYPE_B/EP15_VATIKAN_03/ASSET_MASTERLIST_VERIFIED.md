## Critical historical correction for G01/G02

The production should **not** imply that Gratian himself simply placed the Donation in his original *Decretum*. Scholarship distinguishes the later **Paleae/additions** in D.96 c.13–14 from Gratian's core compilation. This distinction is both historically safer and dramatically useful: the episode can show how an authoritative legal tradition accumulated material over time.

### Print-history warning

Older secondary literature, including Coleman’s introduction, can state that Ulrich von Hutten first printed Valla in 1517. Modern bibliographic scholarship identifies an earlier **Strasbourg 1506 editio princeps**. Hutten’s 1517/1518 publication is still historically important as a later influential circulation point; do not call it the first edition.

### Narration-safe wording

The safest formulation is: **the Donation/Sylvester tradition has Sylvester baptize Constantine in Rome; Eusebius of Caesarea places Constantine’s baptism at the end of his life near Nicomedia. Standard historical accounts identify Eusebius of Nicomedia as the baptizing bishop.** Avoid implying that a single image or a late legend is direct evidence for the historical baptism.

# M. WHAT TO USE FOR “SCIENTIFIC / ESOTERIC” VISUAL ENERGY

This episode does **not** benefit from generic occult symbols, alchemy pages or fabricated “Vatican secrets” imagery unless they are clearly marked as metaphor. The more powerful NOESIS equivalent is **textual forensics**:

1. **Valla marginalia** (B04) — visible correction and comparison.
2. **Valla autograph** (B02) — the investigator’s actual hand.
3. **Donation manuscript rubric** (A01) — documentary authority on parchment.
4. **Paleae / Gratian legal structure** (G01) — how a text enters authoritative transmission.
5. **Constantinian coin/medallion** (E01/E02) — material history contrasted with textual legend.
6. **Medieval and Renaissance Donation scenes** (C04/C02) — the legend becoming visual memory.

If a mystical bridge is needed, use lighting, macro parchment texture, shadow, dust, ink bleed and architectural sound design—not fake sigils or invented manuscript diagrams.

# O. PRODUCTION RIGHTS RED LINES

1. **Do not download Vatican Library / Vatican Museums imagery and assume that age of the artwork makes the digital reproduction free.** BAV professional-use rules require permission for film/TV/multimedia; Vatican Museums website content is separately protected.
2. For **CC BY / CC BY-SA** files, preserve author, source URL and exact license version in the production manifest.
3. For **CC0** Rijksmuseum/NGA/photographer files, still preserve institution/object metadata; it strengthens provenance even where attribution is not legally required.
4. For Commons **PD-Art/PDM** reproductions, retain the Commons file page and source institution URL in the manifest, and perform a final territory check.
5. Never crop an original document in a way that changes meaning. If highlighting words, maintain an unaltered establishing frame first.
